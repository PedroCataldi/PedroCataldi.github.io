# Pedro Cataldi, Ph.D.

### Research Associate · Institute of Astronomy, UNAM

**Extragalactic Astronomy · Galaxy Formation · Cosmology · Cosmological Simulations**

---

## About me

I am a **Research Associate at the Institute of Astronomy (IA–UNAM)** in Mexico City, where I work on galaxy formation, large-scale structure, and cosmological simulations. I am a member of the **Dark Energy Science Collaboration (DESC)** through the Mexico In-Kind contribution, with a focus on connecting simulations and theoretical models with observations from current and upcoming astronomical surveys.

My research interests broadly encompass **galaxy formation and evolution, galaxy clustering, assembly bias, dark matter halos, and alternative cosmological models**. I am particularly interested in using numerical simulations and synthetic observations to understand how galaxies and their environments evolve across cosmic time.

You can find my [institutional profile](https://astronomia.unam.mx/comunidad/cataldi-pedro/) at the Institute of Astronomy, UNAM.

---

## Previous research

Before joining IA–UNAM, I was a **Postdoctoral Researcher in Numerical Astrophysics** at the Institute of Astronomy and Space Physics (IAFE) in Buenos Aires, Argentina, as part of the **GANE** team. There, I worked on numerical approaches to problems involving cosmology, galaxy formation, and baryonic physics.

I completed my **Ph.D. in Physics at the University of Buenos Aires**, under the supervision of **Dr. Susana E. Pedrosa**. My doctoral research focused on alternative cosmological models, baryonic physics, and the evolution of dark matter halos using numerical simulations. My doctoral work was supported by a **CONICET Ph.D. fellowship** and the **LACEGAL-RISE program**.

My [CONICET researcher profile](https://bicyt.conicet.gov.ar/fichas/p/pedro-anselmo-cataldi) is also available online.

## Research

My current research combines **cosmological simulations, galaxy formation, and synthetic observations**, with particular interests in:

- **Galaxy formation and evolution**
- **Galaxy sizes and structural properties**
- **Large-scale structure and galaxy clustering**
- **Halo occupation and galaxy–halo connection**
- **Assembly bias**
- **Alternative gravity and cosmological models**
- **Synthetic observations and mock catalogues**
- **Connections between simulations and upcoming surveys such as Rubin/LSST**
